Argent records the screen of the device to a video file. The agent starts the recording, makes the interaction, and stops the recording. Argent gives you an mp4 file that shows what happened on the screen.

Use a recording when a screenshot is not sufficient. A recording shows an animation, a transition, a gesture, or a sequence of screens. A recording also documents a bug, a flow, or a change of the app for a colleague or a pull request.

## How a recording works

1. The agent brings the app to the state where the video must start.
2. The agent starts the recording on the device.
3. The agent makes the interaction: taps, swipes, navigation, text input.
4. The agent stops the recording.
5. Argent saves the video in the project. The agent tells you the path of the file.

The recording continues while the agent uses other tools. Argent reminds the agent after each tool call that the recording is active. A recording also stops on its own after a time limit. Thus a forgotten recording does not continue forever.

## What the video shows

Argent draws each interaction of the agent into the video. A pulse marks each tap. A trail follows each swipe and each drag. Two markers show the contact points of a pinch or a rotation. Thus the viewer sees where each gesture landed. The agent can also record the raw screen without these markers.

Argent removes the still parts of the recording. When the screen does not change, Argent keeps the first second and removes the rest. Thus a long session with a short activity gives a short video without dead air. The agent can also keep the full real-time recording, for example to measure how long an operation takes on the screen.

The video keeps the size of the screen at the start of the recording. If the screen changes size, Argent keeps the proportions of the new screen and adds black bars. On a foldable simulator, the screen changes size when the other panel shows the UI. To get a panel at full size, tell the agent to start the recording on that panel.

Argent stamps a small Argent watermark into the corner of the video. You can turn the watermark off.

## Remote simulators

Argent also records remote (`remote:`) simulators. The recording is made on the machine that runs the simulator, and the stop call downloads the finished mp4. A large recording therefore takes a moment to transfer.

Four details differ from a local recording:

- Argent removes the still parts and stamps the watermark after the download, in a second pass over the finished video. When `ffmpeg` is not installed, Argent gives you the recording anyway and tells you that it did neither, instead of failing the recording.
- The still parts collapse completely, except the last one, which holds for a second. A local recording keeps the first second of each still part.
- A recording keeps at most 2 GB of video. A longer recording loses its beginning, without a warning.
- A recording of a device in landscape plays sideways. The video keeps the portrait orientation of the screen.

If the download fails, the recording stays on the remote machine until you release the machine. The error message gives the `sim-remote screen-record fetch` command that downloads it.

## Physical iPhones

Argent also records a physical iPhone that is connected to the Mac with a USB cable. Argent captures the screen in the same way as QuickTime Player. Argent installs nothing on the iPhone for the recording.

Three details differ from a simulator recording:

- The first recording on a Mac takes longer. Argent compiles a small capture helper with Xcode and keeps it for the next recordings.
- macOS asks for camera access for `ios-screen-stream` at the first recording. Allow the access. macOS needs it to capture the screen of an iPhone. If you deny it, allow `ios-screen-stream` in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera.
- The video does not show the touch markers.

During a recording, iOS shows a clean status bar with the time 9:41, as it does for QuickTime Player.

## Mac apps

Argent also records [Mac apps](/docs/features/mac-apps), including Mac Catalyst apps. The Argent library in the app records the app window. Thus a recording needs no Screen Recording permission and no `ffmpeg`.

Five details differ from a simulator recording:

- The video shows the app window, with its menus, sheets, popovers and alerts. The video does not show other apps or the desktop.
- The video shows the pointer of the agent. A ring marks each click, and a dot shows a held mouse button.
- Argent keeps recording while the Mac is locked. The video can be less sharp then.
- Argent cannot record a minimized window or a window that is not fully on one display. When this occurs during a recording, the video holds the last frame, and the stop call returns a warning.
- The recording runs in the app and uses some of its processor time. Do not record while the agent profiles the same app.

If the app quits or restarts during a recording, or another Argent tool-server connects to the app, Argent loses the recording. When the agent disconnects Argent from the app during a recording, Argent first asks the app to write the video, and the next stop call says where it is.

## Where the video lands

Argent saves each video in the `.argent/recordings/` directory of the project. The file name contains the device and the time of the recording. Argent does not overwrite an existing file. You can change the directory in the Argent configuration.

The files stay in the working tree. Add the directory to `.gitignore` when you do not want to commit the videos.

## Example instructions

- "Record the login flow from the start screen to the home screen."
- "Record the animation of the bottom sheet when it opens and closes."
- "Record the scroll of the list. I want to see the jank that I reported."
- "Record a video of the checkout for the pull request. Do not show the touch markers."

## Limits

- Argent records iOS simulators, remote simulators, physical iPhones, Android emulators, physical Android devices, and Mac apps. Argent does not record tvOS simulators, Chromium apps, Vega devices, or HarmonyOS devices.
- Argent records a physical iPhone only when the iPhone is connected with a USB cable. Argent cannot record an iPhone that is connected over Wi-Fi.
- One recording per device at a time. Different devices can record at the same time.
- Argent uses `ffmpeg` to encode the video. Install `ffmpeg` before the first recording. On a remote simulator `ffmpeg` is optional: without it the video still comes back, with no watermark and with the still parts left in. A Mac app recording does not use `ffmpeg`.
- On Android, a secure screen such as a password field or a DRM video comes out black.
- A recording needs a stop call before the agent can give you the file. This is also the case when the time limit stopped the recording.

Argent does this with the `screen-recording-start` and `screen-recording-stop` tools. The [tools reference](/docs/reference/tools) contains all tools.
